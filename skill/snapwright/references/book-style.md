# Instruction book style

Goal: someone who has never built this can follow it without guessing.

- One plate level per numbered step; when a level takes more than one picture its pictures
  are sub-steps (12.1, 12.2 ...). Each picture adds at most `max_per_step` parts (kids 4,
  family 7, adult 12, expert 20). Steps are compact regions; colour is a tie-breaker.
- Parts come in bags of about 150 in build order (bags only break between numbered steps).
  Each bag opens with a "parts for this bag" page; every step page has a progress bar with
  the bag number.
- Every callout icon has its quantity and its colour's name (never colour alone). A new part
  the view can't show gets a dashed outline where it goes.
- Each step shows the model so far with new parts outlined in the accent colour, and a
  callout of new parts with quantities. Overhang steps say "press these on from underneath".
- Steps are compact regions: the reader finishes one area before moving to the next.
- The camera changes between plate levels when another quarter view shows clearly more of
  the new parts, or for a single step when two or more of its new parts would be hidden; a
  turn marker shows the rotation.
- A sideways panel is built in a tinted, labelled "sub-build" inset (drawn flat, in its own
  frame), then an attach step shows it on the model, highlighted, with one line of text.
- Cover: finished model, title, parts / height / steps / lots, designer, disclaimer.
- Parts inventory grouped by colour, then the steps, then a finished-model page with four
  views and the software checks, marked "checked in software": every auto-repair is listed
  (highlighted), then weak points and unverified part-colour combos.

Keep our own visual identity. Do not imitate any manufacturer's official instruction layout,
fonts, colour scheme, logo, character mascots or page furniture. No brand names in titles.
